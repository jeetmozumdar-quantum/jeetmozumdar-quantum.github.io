From Local Forgetting to Spatial Attenuation? Reference-Stable Contraction in Partial-SWAP Collision Models.

Author: Jeet Mozumdar

PDF: Download PDF

DOI (Zenodo): https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24100998

ORCID: https://orcid.org/0009-0005-4371-2416

Abstract

We study whether local forgetting in finite-dimensional quantum memory can be composed into attenuation of information across a network. We first prove a reference-stable contraction for repeated partial-SWAP collisions between a system qubit and a memory qubit that is depolarized between collisions: for two joint system--reference states with the same reference marginal, the trace distance contracts over a two-collision block by a factor at most $1-\epsilon$, with $\epsilon=(1-\lambda)^2\sin^2(2\theta)$, uniformly in the reference system and its dimension. This estimate cannot simply be iterated across a branching structure, because the reference marginals required by the hypothesis are not preserved after the first stage. We therefore analyse one explicit branching channel ($\theta=\pi/8$, $\lambda=4/5$) through its numerically recorded Pauli transfer tensor and derive conditional exponential attenuation bounds with rate at most about $0.73$ per generation. These bounds are conditional on the correctness of the recorded transfer tensor and on a depth-three base case. Both have numerical support, but neither has yet been rigorously certified. We also prove, unconditionally, a single-path lower bound showing that the contraction coefficient of the depth-$r$ channel is at least $g^r$ with $g\approx0.68$. The channel is nearly single-path, so it only weakly tests composition, and we identify the missing ingredients for a discriminating test: symmetric children and reuse of memory. A final section reports finite-chain numerical diagnostics and a conjectured probe-mean formula for a reused-memory model; no theorem is claimed there. No Lieb--Robinson-type or spatial attenuation bound is established.
